## Overview

GlassKit started with a simple observation: unreadable glass UI was quietly costing us product designers. People liked the idea, but the experience asked too much of them too early. My goal was to make the product feel obvious within the first minute.

## The problem

We watched product designers try the existing flow. Most hesitated at the same few moments: too many choices, unclear next steps, and feedback that arrived too late. The numbers backed it up: roughly — of people never made it past the first session.

<Img src="/projects/glasskit/research.jpg" alt="Research wall" caption="Mapping where people hesitated during early interviews" />

## My role

As maker, I led the work from research to launch: interviews, flows, interaction design, prototyping and working with engineering on the details that make it feel right.

- Ran interviews and usability sessions
- Designed the end-to-end flows and UI
- Built motion prototypes to test feel, not just layout
- Partnered with engineers on implementation and QA

## Process

We started by cutting. Every screen had to earn its place. Then we rebuilt the core journey around one idea: a kit with contrast-safe glass tokens.

<Img src="/projects/glasskit/flows.jpg" alt="User flows" caption="Before and after: the core flow, simplified" size="wide" />

<Callout type="tip" title="What made the difference">
Prototyping the motion early. Transitions explained the interface better than any copy could.
</Callout>
