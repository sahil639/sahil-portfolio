## Overview

CapX started with a simple observation: onboarding drop-off was quietly costing us first-time traders. People liked the idea, but the experience asked too much of them too early. My goal was to make the product feel obvious within the first minute.

## The problem

We watched first-time traders try the existing flow. Most hesitated at the same few moments: too many choices, unclear next steps, and feedback that arrived too late. The numbers backed it up: roughly 42% of people never made it past the first session.

<Img src="/projects/capx/research.jpg" alt="Research wall" caption="Mapping where people hesitated during early interviews" />

## My role

As lead product designer, I led the work from research to launch: interviews, flows, interaction design, prototyping and working with engineering on the details that make it feel right.

- Ran interviews and usability sessions
- Designed the end-to-end flows and UI
- Built motion prototypes to test feel, not just layout
- Partnered with engineers on implementation and QA

## Process

We started by cutting. Every screen had to earn its place. Then we rebuilt the core journey around one idea: cut onboarding from 9 steps to 4.

<Img src="/projects/capx/flows.jpg" alt="User flows" caption="Before and after: the core flow, simplified" size="wide" />

<Callout type="tip" title="What made the difference">
Prototyping the motion early. Transitions explained the interface better than any copy could.
</Callout>

## What I learned

Simplicity is mostly subtraction, and motion is a design tool, not decoration. Next time I'd bring engineering into prototyping even earlier.
